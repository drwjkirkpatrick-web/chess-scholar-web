# Lesson Plan 38: Counterplay & Defense

> **Module Reference:** Prompt 38 — lesson-38-counterplay-defense.md
> **Duration:** 80 min
> **Age Group:** 12-14
> **Chess Skill Level:** ♖ Rook Tier (1200–1600)
> **Tool:** Physical board + analysis engine

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:
1. **Identify** counter-attacking resources in a defensive position.
2. **Apply** prophylaxis — preventing the opponent's threats before they happen.
3. **Value** defense — understanding that great attackers are also great defenders.

## Key Inquiry Question

> How do you defend when your opponent has the initiative — and why is the best defense often a counter-attack?

## Chess Persona: The King

**Piece:** King  
**Value:** Infinite (the game ends when the king is checkmated)  
**Movement:** One square in any direction  
**Persona Trait:** Surviving Under Fire  
**Why this piece?** The King is the most vulnerable yet most important piece. In defensive positions, the king must sometimes become active — walking up the board to safety or even joining the attack. The King teaches that survival requires both caution and courage. The best defender in history, Tigran Petrosian, was known as "Iron Tigran" for his ability to withstand attacks that would break other players.  
**Historical Note:** Wilhelm Steinitz, the first World Champion, revolutionized chess by proving that defense could be scientific. Before Steinitz, players believed that attack was the only way to win. Steinitz showed that a properly defended position could not be broken — and that counter-attack was the reward for solid defense. His famous quote: "A chess master is someone who knows how to defend."

## Lesson Development

### Introduction (10 min)

**Hook:** Show a position where White appears to be attacking ferociously. Ask: "Black to move. What do you do?" Reveal that Black has a stunning counter-attack that turns the tables.

*Hook questions:*
- "When your opponent is attacking you, what's the worst thing you can do?" *—Panic and only defend passively. The best defense often creates counter-chances.*
- "Why did Tigran Petrosian become World Champion despite playing fewer 'brilliant' attacks than Tal?" *—Because he defended better than anyone. His opponents couldn't break through, and they eventually made mistakes.*
- "What does prophylaxis mean in chess?" *—It means preventing your opponent's threats before they even happen — like vaccinating your position.*

Explain: *Today we learn to defend — and to counter-attack. The King is our guide: when under attack, the king must sometimes walk forward, not hide. Great defenders become great attackers.*

### Step 1: Concept Introduction — Prophylaxis and Counter-Attack (20 min)

**Prophylaxis: Preventing the Threat**

Prophylaxis means asking: "What does my opponent want to do?" and then preventing it.

```
White: Nf3, Bg5, e4, d4, O-O
Black: Nf6, e6, Be7, O-O

White wants to play e5, attacking the knight.
Black plays 1...Nfd7! (prophylaxis — preventing e5 by reinforcing f6)
```

**Counter-Attack: Meeting Threat with Threat**

When defending, creating your own threats forces the attacker to stop and defend.

```
White: Qh5, Bc4, Nf3, d4, O-O
Black: Nf6, e6, Be7, O-O, d5

White appears to be attacking the kingside with Qh5.
Black plays 1...Nc6! (developing with tempo — threatening d4)
2. Be3 dxc4! (counter-attack — now Black is up a pawn and has the initiative)
```

**The Principle: Don't Just Defend — Strike Back**

**Key teaching point:** Passive defense is the last resort. Active defense (creating counter-play) and prophylaxis (preventing threats before they happen) are stronger. Every time you defend, ask: "Can I also create a threat?"

[Student checklist:]
- [ ] I understand what prophylaxis means
- [ ] I can identify my opponent's plan before they execute it
- [ ] I know that the best defense often involves counter-attack

### Step 2: Guided Practice — Defense in Action (20 min)

**Position 1: Defending with Counter-Attack**

```
White: Qh5, Bc4, Nf3, d4, Nb1-c3
Black: Nf6, e6, Be7, O-O, d5, c5

White has played aggressively, but Black's position is sound.
Black to move.

1...cxd4! (opening the c-file for counter-play)
2. Nxd4 Nc6! (attacking the knight and developing with tempo)
3. Nxc6 bxc6 (now Black has open b- and c-files for rooks)
4. Bd3 Rb8! (counter-attack — the b-file is Black's weapon)
```

**Position 2: Prophylactic Thinking**

```
White: e4, d4, Nf3, Bd3, O-O
Black: e5, Nf6, Nc6, Bc5, O-O

White's plan: play d5, attacking the knight on c6.
Black's prophylactic move: 1...a6! (preparing ...Na5 to reinforce c6, or ...b5 to gain space)

This is the Anti-Marshall setup — preventing White's plans before they happen.
```

**Key teaching point:** In both examples, the defender is not just reacting — they are shaping the game. Counter-attack and prophylaxis turn defense into a weapon.

[Checklist for students:]
- [ ] I identified White's plan in Position 1
- [ ] I found Black's counter-attacking resource
- [ ] I understood the prophylactic move in Position 2

### Step 3: Independent Practice — Defense Challenge (20 min)

**Challenge:** Students receive 4 positions where they are under attack. They must find the best defensive resource — either prophylaxis, counter-attack, or active defense.

**Position 1 (Counter-attack available):**
```
White: Qg4, Bc4, Nf3, O-O
Black: Nf6, d5, e6, Be7, O-O

White threatens Qxg7#.
Black to move.

Answer: 1...Ne4! (counter-attack — the knight attacks the queen AND threatens Nxc3, forking queen and rook)
```

**Position 2 (Prophylaxis needed):**
```
White: e4, d4, Nf3, Bg5, c3
Black: e5, Nf6, Nc6, Bc5, O-O

White plans to play d5, then dxc6, opening the center.
Black to move.

Answer: 1...h6! (prophylaxis against Bg5, or 1...a6 to prepare ...Na5)
```

**Position 3 (Active defense — king walk):**
```
White: Qh5, Bc4, Nf3, d4
Black: Nf6, e6, Be7, O-O, d5

White threatens checkmate on h7.
Black to move.

Answer: 1...Kh8! (moving off the diagonal, preparing ...Ng8 if needed)
```

||| Position | Success Metric |
|||------------------------|------------------|
|| Counter-attack | Find the move that attacks back |
|| Prophylaxis | Prevent White's plan before it happens |
|| Active Defense | Improve the position while defending |

[Student checklist:]
- [ ] I found the counter-attack in Position 1
- [ ] I found the prophylactic move in Position 2
- [ ] I found the active defense in Position 3

### Step 4: Sharing & Feedback (10 min)

Learners present their solutions or discuss positions. Use the critique framework:
- **What's working:** "Your counter-attack in Position 1 was excellent — you didn't just block the threat, you created one of your own."
- **One thing to try:** "In Position 2, consider not just what White wants now, but what they want in 3 moves. Prophylaxis looks further ahead."
- **Question:** "If you could only play one defensive move, would you choose prophylaxis or counter-attack? Why?"

## Extended Activity

**Defense Study:** Analyze three games by Tigran Petrosian where he defended a difficult position and won. Write one paragraph per game explaining what made his defense special. Alternatively, analyze a game where you were under attack and find the moment you could have created counter-play.
