# Lesson Plan 21: Opening Principles

> **Module Reference:** Prompt 21 — lesson-21-opening-principles.md  
> **Duration:** 70 min  
> **Age Group:** 10-14  
> **Chess Skill Level:** ♗ Bishop Tier  
> **Tool:** Physical board + PGN viewer / analysis

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:
1. **Apply** the three opening principles: control the center, develop pieces, castle early.
2. **Evaluate** opening moves as good, okay, or bad based on these principles.
3. **Value** preparation — understanding that the opening sets the stage for everything that follows.

## Key Inquiry Question

> How do the first ten moves set the stage for the entire game — and why is controlling the center like building a house on solid ground?

## Chess Persona: The Pawn

**Piece:** Pawn  
**Value:** 1  
**Movement:** One square forward (two on first move), captures diagonally  
**Persona Trait:** Patience & Foundation  
**Why this piece?** The Pawn is the first piece to move in most games, staking a claim in the center. Just as a Pawn slowly builds the foundation of a position, the opening builds the foundation of the entire game. The Pawn teaches us that slow, purposeful steps create lasting strength.  
**Historical Note:** In the original Indian game of chaturanga (around 600 CE), the Pawn could only move one square forward — never two. The two-square first move was added in Europe around the 15th century to speed up the game. This simple change transformed opening strategy forever.

## Lesson Development

### Introduction (5-10 min)

**Hook:** Show a position after 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 (Spanish Opening) versus a position after 1.a4 h5 2.Ra3 Rh6 3.Nh3. Ask students which side looks ready to play a real game.

- *Which side has more pieces off the back rank?* (The Spanish side — White has two pieces developed; the chaotic side has zero useful development.)
- *Which side controls the center?* (The Spanish side — pawns and knights occupy and attack central squares.)
- *Which King looks safer?* (The Spanish side — the King is still in the center but can castle; the chaotic side has exposed the King for no reason.)

Explain: *Today we'll learn the three golden rules of the opening using the Pawn as our guide. The Pawn knows that every great building needs a solid foundation.*

### Step 1: Concept Introduction — The Three Golden Rules (15-20 min)

**Rule 1: Control the Center**

The center consists of the four squares: e4, d4, e5, d5. Controlling these squares with pawns and pieces gives your army room to maneuver and restricts the opponent.

```
After 1.e4 e5 2.d4 exd4:
White controls d4 and e4 with pawns
Black has traded away central control
White now has a space advantage
```

**Rule 2: Develop Your Pieces**

Get your knights and bishops out from the back rank. Each piece should move only once in the opening if possible — don't waste time moving the same piece twice.

```
Good development: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4
Bad development: 1.e4 e5 2.Qh5? Nc6 3.Qxf7#?? (only works against beginners)
```

**Rule 3: Castle Early**

Move your King to safety behind a pawn shield. Castling connects your rooks and gets the King away from the dangerous center.

```
Typical castling: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.d3 Nf6 5.Nc3 d6 6.0-0
Both sides have castled kingside — now the middlegame begins!
```

**Key teaching point:** The opening is not about winning immediately — it is about getting ready to win. Every move should improve your position according to one of these three rules.

### Step 2: Guided Practice — Opening Evaluation Game (15-20 min)

Play through the following moves together. After each pair of moves, students evaluate whether the principles were followed.

```
Game 1: Good Opening
1.e4 e5
2.Nf3 Nc6
3.Bb5 a6
4.Ba4 Nf6
5.0-0 Be7
6.d3 b5
7.Bb3 d6
8.c3 0-0
```

```
Game 2: Bad Opening
1.a3 e5
2.Ra2 Nc6
3.Ra1 Nf6
4.h3 Be7
5.Rh2 0-0
6.Ra1 d5
```

[Checklist for students:]
- [ ] I can identify which side controls the center after each move pair
- [ ] I can count how many pieces each side has developed
- [ ] I can spot when a King castles and explain why it is safer
- [ ] I can explain why Game 1 follows the rules and Game 2 breaks them

**Key teaching point:** Development is a race. The player who finishes development first gets to attack first.

### Step 3: Independent Practice — Opening Builder Challenge (10-15 min)

**Challenge:** Starting from the initial position, each student (or pair) must play the first 8 moves of a game following ALL three principles. Then they trade boards with a partner who checks their work.

||| Position / Criterion | Success Metric |
|||------------------------|------------------|
||| Central pawns advanced (e/d files) | At least one center pawn moved by move 4 |
||| Pieces developed | At least 3 minor pieces off back rank by move 8 |
||| King safety | King castled by move 8 (or castling possible next move) |
||| No repeated moves | No piece moved twice in first 6 moves |

[Student checklist:]
- [ ] I advanced a center pawn in the first four moves
- [ ] I developed at least three minor pieces (knights and bishops)
- [ ] I castled or set up castling by move 8
- [ ] I did not move the same piece twice in the opening
- [ ] My partner agrees I followed all three principles

### Step 4: Sharing & Feedback (5-10 min)

Learners present their openings or discuss positions. Use the critique framework:
- **What's working:** "I like how you controlled the center with pawns on e4 and d4 before developing your pieces — that's exactly the right order."
- **One thing to try:** "Instead of moving that knight again, you could have developed your dark-squared bishop to put more pressure on the center."
- **Question:** "What would you do if your opponent attacked your center pawn — would you defend it or trade it?"

## Assessment

||| Criteria | BE (Below Expectation) | AE (Approaching) | ME (Meets) | EE (Exceeds) |
||----------|------------------------|-------------------|------------|--------------|
||| Control the center | Cannot identify center squares | Can identify center but does not play toward it | Places pawns/pieces to control d4/e4/d5/e5 | Controls center AND restricts opponent's counterplay |
||| Develop pieces | Leaves most pieces on back rank | Develops 1-2 pieces | Develops 3+ minor pieces efficiently | Develops with a plan for where each piece belongs |
||| Castle for safety | Does not castle in first 10 moves | Castles late or unsafely | Castles by move 8 with King safe | Castles early AND improves rook activity simultaneously |
||| Board/Tool Use | Cannot use board/tool | Basic use of board | Competent use with notation | Expert use + analyzes own moves with PGN viewer |

## Extended Activity

**Opening Journal:** Students choose one opening (Italian, Spanish, or Sicilian) and keep a journal of 5 games where they play it. For each game, they write:
1. The first 6 moves
2. Which principle each move follows
3. One thing they would do differently next time

## Differentiation

||| For learners who need more support | For advanced learners |
||-------------------------------------|----------------------|
||| Provide a printed checklist of the three rules to keep beside the board | Challenge them to play the first 10 moves of the Ruy Lopez from memory |
||| Allow them to use a smaller board (5x5) to practice center control concepts | Ask them to explain WHY 1.e4 is better than 1.d4 in terms of piece activity |
||| Pair with a peer who can narrate their reasoning | Give them a position after move 8 and ask them to find the best plan for each side |
