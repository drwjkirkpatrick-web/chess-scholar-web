# Lesson Plan 08: Special Moves: En Passant & Promotion

> *Pawn surprises; the underdog's reward.*

**Chess Skill Level:** ♙ Pawn Tier

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:
1. **Execute** en passant correctly.
2. **Explain** pawn promotion and why the Queen is almost always the best choice.
3. **Value** perseverance — understanding that even the weakest piece can become the strongest.

## Key Inquiry Question

> How can the weakest piece on the board become the strongest — and what does that teach us about never giving up?

## Chess Persona: The Pawn

**Piece:** Pawn  
**Value:** 1 point (but can become 9!)  
**Movement:** Forward one or two squares (from start), captures diagonally; en passant capture; promotion on the 8th rank  
**Persona Trait:** Surprises & Rewards  
**Why this piece?** The Pawn returns as our lesson persona because en passant and promotion are the Pawn's greatest secrets. This lesson teaches that the underdog — the weakest, slowest piece — holds two of the most surprising and powerful weapons in chess. The Pawn who reaches the other side transforms into a Queen. This is the ultimate lesson in perseverance: small steps, taken consistently, lead to extraordinary rewards.  
**Historical Note:** Pawn promotion has existed since chaturanga, but the rules varied wildly. In some medieval versions, a Pawn could only promote to the piece of the file it reached (a Pawn on the a-file or h-file could only become a Rook). In 13th-century Italy, some players ruled that a Pawn could not promote to a Queen if the original Queen was still on the board — it had to become a different piece! The modern rule (promote to any piece, almost always Queen) was standardized by the 19th century. En passant appeared later: it was added in the 15th century alongside the two-square Pawn move to prevent Pawns from bypassing each other too easily.

## Lesson Development

### Introduction (5-10 min)

**Hook:** Place a White Pawn on a7. Ask: "If this Pawn moves one more step forward, what happens?"

- "Can a Pawn become a Queen?" *Yes — that is called promotion, and it is one of the coolest rules in chess!*
- "Can a Pawn capture an enemy Pawn that just ran past it?" *Yes — that is called en passant, and it is the sneakiest rule in chess!*
- "Why do you think the weakest piece gets the biggest reward?" *Because it had to work the hardest to get there.*

Explain: *Today we learn the Pawn's two secret weapons: en passant (the sneak attack) and promotion (the grand transformation). The Pawn teaches us that never giving up leads to miracles.*

### Step 1: Concept Introduction — En Passant (15-20 min)

**Teach en passant:**
- En passant is a **special Pawn capture** that can only happen immediately after an enemy Pawn moves two squares from its starting position.
- If your Pawn could have captured the enemy Pawn had it moved only **one square** instead of two, you have the **option** to capture it en passant.
- The capture is written as if the Pawn moved only one square (e.g., exd6e.p. or simply exd6).
- You **must** capture en passant on the very next move, or you lose the right forever.

**Demonstration:**

```
Starting position:
White Pawn on e5.
Black Pawn on d7.
```

Black plays 1...d7-d5 (two squares). The Black Pawn jumps past d6.

White can capture en passant: exd6. The White Pawn moves to d6 and removes the Black Pawn from d5.

```
After en passant:
White Pawn on d6.
Black Pawn removed from d5.
```

**Why does this rule exist?** When the two-square Pawn move was introduced in the 15th century, players realized Pawns could bypass each other. En passant was added so a Pawn cannot escape capture by jumping past an enemy Pawn.

**Key teaching point:** En passant only works:
1. When an enemy Pawn moves TWO squares from its starting position.
2. When your Pawn is on the 5th rank (White) or 4th rank (Black).
3. On the very next move only — it expires!

### Step 2: Concept Introduction — Promotion (part of same 15-20 min block)

**Teach promotion:**
- When a Pawn reaches the **final rank** (rank 8 for White, rank 1 for Black), it **must** be promoted.
- The Pawn is replaced by any piece of the same color: Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight.
- **Almost always, choose the Queen.** (Under-promotion to a Knight, Rook, or Bishop is rare and only used in special tactics.)
- You can have **multiple Queens** on the board — there is no limit.

**Demonstration:**

```
White Pawn on a7.
Black King on h8.
```

White plays 1.a8=Q. The Pawn becomes a Queen on a8.

**Notation:** a8=Q (or a8Q). Some write a8/Q.

**Why promote to Queen?** The Queen is worth 9 points — nine times more powerful than a Pawn. Promoting to anything else is usually a mistake unless a Knight is needed for a specific checkmate pattern.

**Key teaching point:** A Pawn that reaches the end of the board is like a student who graduates — it transforms into something far greater. Never underestimate a Pawn marching toward promotion.

### Step 3: Guided Practice — Pawn Surprise Scenarios (15-20 min)

Students work in pairs.

**Activity Part 1 — En Passant Detective:**
Set up the following positions and ask: "Can White capture en passant?"

```
Scenario 1:
White Pawn on e5. Black Pawn on d7. Black just played d7-d5. → YES! exd6.

Scenario 2:
White Pawn on e5. Black Pawn on d6. → NO — the Pawn did not just move two squares.

Scenario 3:
White Pawn on e4. Black Pawn on d7. Black just played d7-d5. → NO — White's Pawn is on the 4th rank, not the 5th.

Scenario 4:
White Pawn on e5. Black Pawn on f7. Black just played f7-f5. → YES! exf6.

Scenario 5:
White Pawn on e5. Black Pawn on d7. Black played d7-d5 TWO moves ago, and White moved a Knight in between. → NO — en passant expired!
```

**Activity Part 2 — Promotion Race:**
Set up a Pawn each on a2 (White) and h7 (Black). Remove all other pieces. Both Pawns race to promote. White moves first. Who wins? What if Black gets a head start with the Pawn on h5?

```
Race 1:
White Pawn a2 vs Black Pawn h7. White moves first.
White promotes on move 6 (a3, a4, a5, a6, a7, a8=Q).
Black promotes on move 6 (h6, h5, h4, h3, h2, h1=Q).
But White moves first, so White promotes first and can capture Black's new Queen!
```

[Checklist for students:]
- [ ] I can identify when en passant is available.
- [ ] I remember that en passant only works on the very next move.
- [ ] I know that a Pawn promotes when it reaches the final rank.
- [ ] I choose the Queen for promotion (unless told otherwise).
- [ ] I write down en passant and promotion moves in notation.

### Step 4: Independent Practice — Underdog's Triumph Challenge (10-15 min)

**Challenge:** Set up this position:

```
White: King on e1, Pawn on d7.
Black: King on e8, Pawn on e7, Rook on a8.
```

**Task 1:** White to move. Can White promote the d7 Pawn? What square? What piece? Write the move in notation.

**Task 2:** After White promotes, Black plays ...e7-e5 (two squares!). Can White capture en passant on the next move? Show the move and the resulting position.

**Task 3:** Is White winning now? Explain why promotion and en passant together turned a losing position into a winning one.

||| Position / Criterion | Success Metric |
|||------------------------|------------------|
|| Student promotes correctly | Understands promotion mechanics |
|| Student executes en passant correctly | Understands en passant timing and execution |
|| Student evaluates the position after both special moves | Understands how special moves change the game |

[Student checklist:]
- [ ] I set up the position correctly.
- [ ] I promoted the Pawn and chose the right piece.
- [ ] I captured en passant on the very next move.
- [ ] I explained how the position changed.
- [ ] I wrote all moves in proper notation.

### Step 4: Sharing & Feedback (5-10 min)

Learners present their solutions. Use the critique framework:
- **What's working:** "You remembered that en passant only works immediately — that is the trickiest part of the rule!"
- **One thing to try:** "Try under-promoting to a Knight instead of a Queen. When might a Knight be better than a Queen?"
- **Question:** "If there were no promotion rule, would anyone ever push their Pawns to the end of the board? Why is the reward necessary?"

## Extended Activity

**Pawn's Journey Story:** Students write a short story from the Pawn's perspective about marching across the board, surviving dangers, and transforming into a Queen.

**Physical Activity:** "Promotion Ceremony" — students line up as Pawns and "march" (step forward one at a time) to a finish line. When they reach it, they receive a crown and become "Queens."

**Digital Activity:** Practice en passant and promotion on lichess.org/learn.

**Teaching Activity:** Teach a family member both special moves and quiz them with a "Can you capture en passant here?" test.

## Differentiation

||| For learners who need more support | For advanced learners |
||-------------------------------------|----------------------|
|| Use a visual timer to show that en passant "expires" after one move | Introduce rare under-promotion puzzles (promote to Knight for a fork, etc.) |
|| Practice en passant with only one pair of Pawns at a time | Challenge them to find a position where promoting to a Rook is better than Queen |
|| Create a "promotion checklist" card: "Did your Pawn reach the end? Pick Queen!" | Ask them to explain why stalemate can sometimes result from over-promoting |
