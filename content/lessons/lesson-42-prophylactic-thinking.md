## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:
1. **Identify** the opponent's plan before they execute it.
2. **Create** moves that prevent the opponent's threats rather than just reacting to them.
3. **Value** prevention — understanding that stopping a problem before it starts is better than solving it after.

## Key Inquiry Question

> How do you know what your opponent wants before they do it — and why is preventing their plan more powerful than reacting to it?

## Chess Persona: The King

**Piece:** King  
**Value:** Infinite  
**Movement:** One square in any direction  
**Persona Trait:** Prevention & Foresight  
**Why this piece?** The King does not attack; he survives. His value is infinite because the entire game is about his safety. Prophylactic thinking is the King's wisdom: the best way to win is to prevent your opponent from ever launching their attack. The King teaches that defense is not passive — it is the art of taking away your opponent's future.  
**Historical Note:** Aron Nimzowitsch formalized prophylaxis in his 1925 masterpiece *My System*. He coined the term "prophylactic thinking" and demonstrated that moves which restrict the opponent — like a well-timed a2-a4 to stop ...b5 — could be as valuable as direct attacking moves. Tigran Petrosian, World Champion 1963–1969, became the greatest prophylactic player in history; he once said, "I don't believe in psychology. I believe in good moves." His "good moves" were often quiet preventions.

## Lesson Development

### Introduction (5-10 min)

**Hook:** Set up a position where Black is about to play ...Nd4 forking Queen and Bishop. Ask: "White to move. You can defend the fork after it happens, or you can prevent it now. What move prevents the fork?"

```
Position: White Ke1, Qd1, Bc4, Nf3, pawns a2,b2,c2,d4,e4,f2,g2,h2
          Black Ke8, Qd8, Bc5, Nf6, pawns a7,b7,c7,d6,e5,f7,g7,h7
```

- *What is Black's threat?* — *...Nd4 forking Queen and Bishop.*
- *Can you stop it before it happens?* — *Yes — Qe2 or Bb3 or c3 remove the landing square or protect the targets.*
- *Which is more powerful: preventing the fork or letting it happen and then recapturing?* — *Prevention saves time and keeps initiative.*

Explain: *Today we learn the highest-level skill in chess: prophylaxis — thinking about your opponent's plans and stopping them before they begin. The King is our guide because his survival depends on prevention.*

### Step 1: Concept Introduction — What Does Your Opponent Want? (15-20 min)

Teach the three-step prophylactic method:

1. **Find the opponent's best plan:** Ask, "If it were their turn, what would they do?" Look for pawn breaks, piece repositioning, and king safety improvements.
2. **Identify the move that makes that plan impossible or harder:** Sometimes it is a direct prevention (h3 to stop ...Bg4); sometimes it is a restraint (a4 to stop ...b5).
3. **Evaluate whether your prevention is worth more than your own active plan:** Prophylaxis is not always best — but it is the move most beginners never consider.

Example position — Petrosian-style prophylaxis:

```
White: Kg1, Qd2, Re1, Be3, Nf3, Bg2, pawns a2,b2,c2,d4,e4,f2,g3,h2
Black: Kg8, Qd8, Re8, Be7, Nf6, Bb7, pawns a7,b7,c6,d5,e6,f7,g7,h7

Key move: a2-a4 (stopping ...b5, which would undermine White's c4-pawn and activate the Bb7)
```

This move does not attack, but it takes away Black's counterplay for the next 10 moves.

**Key teaching point:** *The strongest players don't just ask "What is my best move?" They ask "What does my opponent want?" and then ask "How do I stop it?"*

### Step 2: Guided Practice — The Prophylactic Pause (15-20 min)

Show positions on the PGN viewer. Pause after White's move and ask: "Before you move, what does Black want?"

Position 1: Black wants to play ...f5 in a King's Indian. White plays h3 (stops ...Ng4), Re1 (prepares e4-e5), and only then advances.

```
1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 Bg7 4.e4 d6 5.Nf3 O-O 6.Be2 e5 7.O-O Nc6 8.d5 Ne7 9.Ne1 Nd7 10.Nd3 f5 11.exf5 gxf5 12.f3
```

Position 2: Black wants ...b5 in a Ruy Lopez. White plays a4 immediately.

```
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 Nf6 5.O-O Be7 6.Re1 b5 7.Bb3 d6 8.c3 O-O 9.h3 Nb8 10.d4 Nbd7 11.Nbd2 Bb7 12.Bc2 Re8 13.Nf1 Bf8 14.Ng3 g6 15.a4
```

[Checklist for students:]
- [ ] I identified the opponent's most dangerous plan (pawn break or piece activation)
- [ ] I found a move that makes that plan harder or impossible
- [ ] I explained whether my prevention was better than an active attacking move
- [ ] I recognized that prophylaxis is not retreat — it is controlling the future

### Step 3: Independent Practice — Prevention Challenge (10-15 min)

**Challenge:** Students are given three positions where the opponent has a clear plan. They must:
1. State the opponent's plan
2. Find the prophylactic move
3. Explain why prevention is stronger than reaction

||| Position / Criterion | Success Metric |
|||------------------------|------------------|
|| Opponent's plan identified | 3/3 positions |
|| Prophylactic move found | 2/3 positions |
|| Strategic explanation given | Verbal/written |

[Student checklist:]
- [ ] I named the opponent's plan in words before looking for my move
- [ ] I considered at least two candidate moves and chose the preventive one
- [ ] I justified my choice by explaining what my opponent can no longer do

### Step 4: Sharing & Feedback (5-10 min)

Pairs share their prevention moves. Use the critique framework:
- **What's working:** "You clearly identified that Black wanted ...d5 to free their Bishop. Your Re1-d1 was a subtle prevention."
- **One thing to try:** "Consider also what happens if you allow ...d5 but are ready to meet it with e4-e5 — sometimes the best prophylaxis is a better threat."
- **Question:** "If your opponent switches plans after your preventive move, what is your next step?"

## Extended Activity

**The Prophylactic Diary:** For the next week, play at least three games (online or physical). After each game, write down one moment where you identified your opponent's plan and either prevented it or failed to. Include the move you played and the move you now think was prophylactic. Bring your diary to the next lesson.
